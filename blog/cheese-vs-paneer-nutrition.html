Same Protein. Very Different Salt Shaker

Per 100 g, paneer and processed cheese carry about the same protein. The cheese carries about 90 times the sodium, and that is the number worth watching.

By Nikhil Ballal

The 5 minute version

Cheese vs paneer is a comparison of two dairy foods that sit next to each other on Indian menus and behave very differently on a nutrition table. Per 100 g, they carry about the same protein. Processed cheese carries about double the fat and about 90 times the sodium.

Paneer is the leaner pick, as long as it is actually paneer.

Everyone argues about protein. The protein is a tie. The story is the salt.

Paneer arrives unseasoned. Processed cheese arrives seasoned for the week.

How do cheese and paneer compare per 100 g?

Per 100 g, paneer has 18.9 g of protein, 14.8 g of fat and 18 mg of sodium by India's food composition table, and US processed cheese has 18.1 g of protein, 30.7 g of fat and 1,671 mg of sodium by the USDA. Side by side:

Why are the cheese numbers from the USDA and not India's table?

The cheese numbers are from the USDA because India's table, IFCT 2017 from the National Institute of Nutrition, has no processed cheese entry. In the open, machine readable copy of IFCT 2017 that I checked, the word cheese appears exactly once, as "cottage cheese", which is listed as another name for paneer itself. Its dairy neighbours are paneer and khoa.

So the cheese column is USDA FoodData Central record 170853: American pasteurised process cheese, fortified with vitamin D. It is not Nandini cheese and I am not going to pretend it is. It is a well documented stand in, and you should know that before you quote it.

Does paneer have more protein than cheese?

Paneer has slightly more protein than cheese per 100 g, but only by 0.8 g. My maths: 18.9 minus 18.1.

That is a tie dressed up as a win. If protein is your reason for choosing one, you can stop choosing.

Is processed cheese really 90 times saltier than paneer?

By these two tables, yes, about 90 times. My maths: 1,671 mg divided by 18 mg is 92.6, which I am rounding down to "about 90" so nobody mistakes it for a lab result.

If you remember one number from this post, make it the salt. Not the protein, and not even the fat. Two foods that look interchangeable on a menu are separated by more than 1.6 g of sodium per 100 g. My maths: 1,671 minus 18 is 1,653 mg.

One honest caveat. The 18 mg is plain paneer. A paneer tikka marinade or a butter masala gravy decides how salty the finished dish is, and neither table covers that.

Does processed cheese have double the fat of paneer?

Processed cheese has about double the fat of paneer per 100 g. My maths: 30.7 divided by 14.8 is 2.07. Paneer is not a low fat food at 14.8 g. It is simply the lower of the two.

What about calories in cheese vs paneer?

Calories in cheese vs paneer depend on the source, so I am treating them carefully. The USDA lists 366 kcal per 100 g for its US processed cheese. IFCT 2017 lists paneer's energy as 1,079 kJ per 100 g, which is about 258 kcal. My maths: 1,079 divided by 4.184.

Different labs, different methods, different products. A Nandini pack may print something else. I could not open a nutrition panel for the Nandini cheese block, so I am not printing a Nandini number.

Is cheese more expensive than paneer?

Cheese is more expensive than paneer only for some packs. On bbnow on 3 Nov 2026, the Nandini Processed Cheese Block in a 200 g carton was ₹120, which works out to ₹60 per 100 g. The Nandini Fresh Paneer 200 g pouch was ₹96, which is ₹48 per 100 g (my maths: 96 divided by 2).

The Nandini Cheese Slice pouch of 750 g, 50 slices, was ₹366. That is about ₹49 per 100 g (my maths: 366 divided by 7.5 is 48.8). So the block is about 25 percent dearer than the paneer per 100 g (my maths: 60 divided by 48 is 1.25), and the slices cost about the same as the paneer. Prices on bbnow can vary by delivery pincode.

"Cheese costs more" is true of one carton. It is not a law of dairy.

What should diners and restaurants do with this?

  • If you are watching salt, swapping processed cheese for paneer is the big move. It matters far more than the protein difference.
  • If you are chasing protein, do not pay extra for either. Per 100 g they are tied.
  • Compare price per 100 g, not price per pack. The carton and the slices tell different stories.
  • Restaurants should say which one is in the dish. "Cheese" and "paneer" are not interchangeable words on a menu.

Is the paneer on your plate actually paneer?

Everything above assumes the paneer is made from milk. Karnataka's Food Safety division issued a notification on 17 Aug 2026 banning the manufacture, storage and sale of analogue paneer, the non-milk substitute, for one year. Processed cheese is excluded because it has its own FSSAI standard. The state also said its tested samples showed no adulteration; the ban is precautionary.

I covered the ban itself in Your Paneer May Not Be Paneer, and the same naming problem shows up in misleading 100 percent food claims. For the same kind of comparison at breakfast, see idli vs dosa calories.

Edible Factor lists both on the same dish, calories beside price, where the restaurant has published them, because the comparison you actually make at the counter is both at once.

Paneer and processed cheese tie on protein. They do not tie on salt.

The salt shaker was inside the cheese all along.
