Chai Wins on Calories, and Coffee Has the Milk to Blame

By ICMR-NIN's recipes, a cup of coffee carries 35 kcal more than a cup of chai, and more caffeine too. The sugar is the same in both, so the coffee itself is not the culprit.

By Nikhil Ballal

The 4 minute version

Coffee vs chai calories is the comparison of the energy in one cup of each, and by ICMR-NIN's recipes chai is the lighter cup. A cup of coffee made with 2 teaspoons of sugar and 100 ml of milk, as I read the table, is about 110 kcal. A cup of chai made with 2 teaspoons of sugar and 50 ml of toned milk is about 75 kcal. The gap is 35 kcal a cup.

Coffee also carries more caffeine. Put those two facts together and coffee sounds like the villain of the morning.

It is not, quite. The two recipes use the same sugar. What changes is the milk.

Which has more calories, coffee or chai?

Coffee has more: about 110 kcal a cup against about 75 kcal for chai, by the NIN recipes.

Both numbers come from Annexure 8 of the Dietary Guidelines for Indians, the 2011 manual from ICMR's National Institute of Nutrition in Hyderabad. Annexure 8 lists approximate calories for cooked foods and drinks at a standard portion. Here the portion is one cup, and NIN's cup is 200 ml.

The table calls the second drink "Tea". Chai is my word for it, not NIN's.

Why is coffee higher in calories than chai?

Because the coffee recipe has 100 ml where the tea recipe has 50 ml of milk, and that is the only listed difference.

The coffee row reads 2 teaspoons of sugar plus 100 ml. It does not say what the 100 ml is. The tea row right next to it says 50 ml of toned milk. Reading the coffee's 100 ml as milk is the natural reading, but it is my reading, and the table does not say which milk.

The sugar is identical, 2 teaspoons in each. The table also does not list milk on its own here, so I cannot split the 35 kcal exactly.

What I can say plainly: put the same milk and the same sugar in both cups and most of that gap should close. That is my reading of two recipes, not a number NIN prints. Coffee has the milk to blame.

Is this the same as South Indian filter coffee?

No. This is NIN's standard coffee recipe, not specifically filter coffee.

A tumbler of filter coffee at a darshini can have more milk, less milk, more sugar or a stronger decoction than the NIN cup. Treat 110 kcal as the standard recipe, not a reading of your tumbler. Filter coffee loyalists may now put down the davara.

How many extra calories is three cups of coffee a day?

Three cups of coffee come to about 105 kcal more than three cups of chai, by our sum.

The working: 3 cups of coffee at 110 kcal is 330 kcal. 3 cups of chai at 75 kcal is 225 kcal. 330 minus 225 is 105 kcal a day.

How much caffeine is in coffee and tea?

A 150 ml cup of brewed coffee has 80 to 120 mg of caffeine, and tea has 30 to 65 mg, according to NIN.

These ranges are in the Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, repeated unchanged from 2011. Instant coffee sits lower, at 50 to 65 mg. Note the cup: the caffeine figures are per 150 ml, while the calorie table uses a 200 ml cup.

How much caffeine is too much in a day?

NIN's 2024 guidelines put the tolerable limit at 300 mg of caffeine a day, and ask for tea and coffee in moderation so you stay under it.

The number is new in 2024. The 2011 text gave none.

My maths, on the 150 ml cups NIN uses for caffeine: three cups of brewed coffee at the top of the range is 3 × 120 = 360 mg, over the limit. At the bottom it is 3 × 80 = 240 mg, under it. Three cups of tea at the top of its range is 3 × 65 = 195 mg.

Does ICMR-NIN say to drink tea instead of coffee?

Not any more: NIN's 2011 guidelines advised preferring tea over coffee, and the 2024 edition drops that advice.

The 2024 beverage points cover water, whole fruit over juice, buttermilk, coconut water and lemon water, no soft drinks and no alcohol. There is nothing in them on tea against coffee.

That advice is a 2011 line. The current guidance is about how much caffeine, not which cup.

Two other points in the 2024 text are worth knowing, as NIN's guidance and not mine. New in 2024, it says tea's benefits "can be best obtained if milk is not added to tea". Carried over from 2011, it advises avoiding tea and coffee for at least one hour before and after meals, because tannins can interfere with iron absorption.

What should coffee and chai drinkers do?

  • Count cups, not drinks. Three cups a day is a different number from one.
  • Look at the milk first if you want a lighter cup. The sugar in the NIN recipes is the same in both.
  • Keep caffeine under 300 mg a day, which NIN's 2024 guidelines give as the tolerable limit.
  • Leave an hour around meals before and after tea or coffee, per NIN.
  • Treat these as standard recipes. Your tumbler, your milk and your sugar will differ.

The cup is also getting more expensive. I wrote about why a Bengaluru cup of coffee or tea now costs more at several hotels, and the Nandini milk price hike behind it. The 2 teaspoons of sugar have their own story in the sugar price rise.

That is why Edible Factor puts a drink's calories next to its price, where the restaurant has published them. The milk shows up on both.

The coffee was never the heavy part of the cup. The milk jug was.

Chai wins on calories. Coffee wins on staying awake. The milk wins on both.
