Two Thalis Walk Into a Calorie Table

Built from one government nutrition guide, a South meals plate and a North roti plate land just 80 kcal apart. The bigger gap is the bill, and it depends on whether you eat at home or out.

By Nikhil Ballal

The 5 minute version

South Indian vs North Indian thali calories are the energy in the two regional plates, and built from one government guide they come out only 80 kcal apart: a South meals plate is 670 kcal and a North roti plate is 750 kcal, by my maths. I built both plates from the ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition's Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2011): Annexure 8 for the cooked dishes and Annexure 14 for the curd. Nearly all of the gap is the cereal.

So the regional rivalry is mostly a rice and roti question. The bigger difference is where you eat the plate, and what it costs there.

Which has more calories, a South Indian or a North Indian thali?

On the two plates I built, the North roti plate has more: 750 kcal against 670 kcal, a gap of 80 kcal. Those totals are our sums, not figures NIN publishes.

Eighty kcal is exactly one phulka.

Real thalis vary wildly with ghee, papad, sweets and refills. These are the plates I built from the table, not a survey of what restaurants serve.

How did I build the two plates?

I took every number from one NIN document: Annexure 8 for the cooked dishes and Annexure 14 for the curd, so the two plates are measured with the same ruler. NIN's cup is 200 ml.

The working, in full. South: 340 + 110 + 150 + 70 = 670. North: 240 + 170 + 100 + 170 + 70 = 750.

Two choices are hiding in there, and I would rather you see them. First, the dal is half a cup, because that is NIN's serving, while the sambar is a full cup. Second, the table has no "poriyal" or "sabzi" row. It has generic vegetable rows, dry and with gravy. Matching dry to poriyal and gravy to sabzi was my choice.

Is a North Indian thali much heavier than a South Indian one?

No: on the two plates I built from one ICMR-NIN guide, the North roti plate is only 80 kcal heavier, 750 against 670 (our sums).

Once you line up the plates, 70 of the 80 kcal is cereal: 410 against 340. The pulse and vegetable together come to 270 on the North plate (100 + 170) and 260 on the South plate (110 + 150). That is a 10 kcal gap. The curd is the same 70 on both.

So the table does not say North Indian food is heavier. It says three phulkas plus a cup of rice is more cereal than two cups of rice. That is arithmetic, not culture.

What if the sabzi is dry?

Then the North plate drops to 730 kcal and the gap shrinks to 60, by my maths. Swap the gravy row (170) for the dry row (150) and you take 20 off: 750 minus 20 = 730, and 730 minus 670 = 60.

Why is the curd counted as 70 kcal?

Because 70 kcal per 100 ml is NIN's portion value for milk and milk products made with toned milk. It sits in Annexure 14 of the 2011 guidelines, not Annexure 8 with the other rows, and there is no separate curd row anywhere in the document.

So the curd is a milk-products value, not one measured for curd.

How much does a thali cost at home versus eating out?

A home-cooked veg thali cost ₹30.80 a plate in September 2026, according to CRISIL's Roti Rice Rate as reported by Trak.in. That is up about 10% on a year earlier, from ₹28.10.

That ₹30.80 is an India average, worked out from input prices in north, south, east and west India, and it is CRISIL's own veg thali, not either plate in my table.

Eating out is a different scale. In May 2026, Oneindia reported that a plate meal at several darshini-style hotels in Bengaluru went from ₹90 to ₹100 after commercial LPG rose, and that sit-down hotels were higher.

My maths: ₹100 ÷ ₹30.80 is about 3.2, so the darshini plate is a little over three times the home plate. The calorie gap between regions was 80 kcal. The price gap between home and out is about ₹70. One of these deserves the argument.

Will the thali get more expensive after the milk hike?

No new meals price has been reported yet. PC Rao, a Bengaluru hotel association head quoted by The Hans India on 31 October, said hotels plan phased rises on milk-based items after the ₹8 a litre Nandini milk hike. That is a plan, not a price in force, though some hotels have since added ₹3 to ₹5 to a cup of coffee or tea.

So the ₹100 plate is a May price, from before the milk hike. CRISIL's October 2026 figure had not come out by 3 November, so ₹30.80 is still the latest.

How should you compare thalis when you eat out?

  • Count the cereal first. Rice and roti decide most of the gap.
  • If you want the cheapest plate in the comparison, it is the one at home.

If breakfast is your battleground instead, I did the same exercise for idli vs dosa. The cup next to the thali gets its own table in coffee vs chai, and the curd on both plates now sits behind the Nandini milk price hike.
