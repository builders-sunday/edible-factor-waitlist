Your Filter Coffee Just Got a ₹5 Upgrade You Did Not Order

Several Bengaluru hotels put coffee and tea up by ₹3 to ₹5 a cup on 2 November 2026, a day after Nandini milk got dearer. There is still no single new price for a coffee in this city.

By Nikhil Ballal

The 5 minute version

The Bengaluru coffee price hike is the ₹3 to ₹5 a cup that several Bengaluru hotels added to coffee and tea from 2 November 2026, the day after the Nandini milk price went up. It is not a citywide rule. Each hotel set its own price, and some had not moved yet.

The milk hike reached the counter within a day. It just did not reach every counter the same way.

The decoction is exactly as strong as last week. Only the rate board got stronger.

How much more does coffee cost in Bengaluru now?

At the hotels that moved, coffee and tea went up by ₹3 to ₹5 a cup. Vishwavani reported on 2 November that several hotels raised both by ₹5 that day.

Pratidhvani, also on 2 November, reported these prices at some hotels: a mini coffee or tea from ₹15 to ₹20, a regular cup from ₹20 to ₹25, and jaggery coffee from ₹35 to ₹40. Kannada News Today separately reported the same mini and regular figures. Neither report names the hotels, so read these as reported prices, not one menu.

The same Pratidhvani report says the rise at some Bengaluru hotels was ₹3 to ₹5, and that the rest were still preparing to revise.

Is there one new price for a coffee in Bengaluru?

No: hotels that moved raised coffee and tea by ₹3 to ₹5 a cup, and there is no single citywide price. "₹5 more" is what several hotels did. It is not what coffee in Bengaluru now costs.

The starting prices were never the same either. In September 2026, some Bengaluru hotels had already raised coffee and tea by ₹2 to ₹3, according to The Federal Karnataka: ₹20 to ₹30 at some hotels became ₹22 to ₹33, and ₹15 at small hotels and tea shops became ₹16 to ₹18. On 31 October, IANS put tea and coffee at ₹20 to ₹48 a cup across Bengaluru hotels.

So a ₹5 rise lands on a ₹15 cup and a ₹40 cup alike. On the ₹15 cup that is a third more, by my maths (₹5 ÷ ₹15). On the ₹40 cup it barely registers. Anyone quoting "the new price of coffee" is quoting one hotel.

Why did Bengaluru hotels raise coffee and tea prices?

Milk is the trigger. Nandini toned milk went from ₹46 to ₹54 a litre on 1 November, and I have broken the rate card down in what the Nandini milk price hike means.

It is not the only cost hotel owners mention. In the Vishwavani report they also point to rice, pulses, edible oil and commercial gas. Karnataka Hotels Association president G K Shetty told Asianet Newsable that hotel grade rice is now around ₹83 a kg, against about ₹62 earlier. Shetty also warned that a cup of tea could become around ₹5 more expensive. That was a warning, not a price list.

How much will a ₹5 coffee rise cost me in a month?

If you drink two coffees a day at a hotel that raised by ₹5, you will pay about ₹300 more a month. That is my maths: 2 coffees × ₹5 × 30 days = ₹300.

At a hotel that raised by ₹3, the same habit costs 2 × ₹3 × 30 = ₹180 more, also my maths. Which hotel you walk into now matters more than it did last month.

Will idli and dosa prices go up in Bengaluru too?

Possibly, but not yet. Vishwavani reported on 2 November that hotel owners are preparing a 5 to 10 percent rise on idli, dosa, rice bath and other tiffin and meals. That is a plan.

As of 3 November I found no report of idli or dosa prices actually raised anywhere because of the milk hike. If your dosa costs more this week, that is your hotel's decision, not a city announcement.

Which Bengaluru restaurants said they would revise prices?

Two named restaurants told Deccan Herald on 30 October, before the hike took effect, that they were considering it. Halli Mane in Malleswaram said it was likely to revise menu prices the following week. MTR said it was considering a revision, with the extent still undecided.

I found no later report of what either one actually did. "Considering" is the whole of what is on record.

Why do the hotel associations' estimates not match?

Because they are different bodies giving different guesses. Subramanya Holla of the Bangalore Hotels Association estimated menu prices may rise 5 to 8 percent. PC Rao of the Bruhat Bengaluru Hotels Association expected 10 to 15 percent. G K Shetty of the Karnataka Hotels Association warned of about ₹5 on tea.

Three associations, three estimates, and no single price anywhere in them. That is normal. Each hotel buys its own milk and sets its own board.

What should coffee drinkers and hotels do now?

  • Diners: read the rate board before you order, even at your regular place. This week it may have changed.
  • Diners: compare two nearby hotels. A ₹3 rise and a ₹5 rise are a ₹120 a month difference on two cups a day, by my maths.
  • Diners: treat any "dosa is now costlier" message as unconfirmed until your hotel's menu says so.
  • Hotels: if you raise the price, put the new number where people can see it before they order. Restaurants can publish a price change themselves on Plate.
  • Hotels: if you held your price, say that too. It is the one piece of news a regular wants to hear.

If you want the other half of the cup, coffee vs chai compares the two on calories and caffeine. And breakfast has its own surprise in idli vs dosa calories, before anyone reprices either.

Edible Factor shows a dish's price next to its calories, where the restaurant has published them. A ₹5 change reads differently when you can see what is in the cup.
