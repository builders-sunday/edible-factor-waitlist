Nandini Milk Just Got ₹8 More Expensive

Nandini toned milk went from ₹46 to ₹54 a litre on 1 November 2026. Every coffee, curd and sweet made with it in Karnataka now has a new cost underneath.

By Nikhil Ballal

The 5 minute version

The Nandini milk price hike is the Karnataka Milk Federation's new rate card, in force from 1 November 2026, which raised toned milk from ₹46 to ₹54 a litre. Every milk variant on the card went up by ₹8 to ₹12 a litre, and curd went up by more.

The government says most of the money goes to dairy farmers. That is the point of the hike. It is also why it will not stay inside the milk packet.

The cow, as usual, was not consulted.

What exactly did KMF change on 1 November?

KMF raised the price of every Nandini milk variant on its rate card, effective 1 November 2026. The Hindu printed the card on 31 October. These are the per litre prices, old to new:

Nandini rate card, per litre (curd per 1 kg pouch), from 1 November 2026
VariantOldNewRise
Toned₹46₹54₹8
Homogenised toned₹47₹56₹9
Homogenised cow₹50₹58₹8
Special toned₹52₹64₹12
Shubham₹52₹64₹12
Homogenised Shubham₹53₹65₹12
Curd, 1 kg₹54₹68₹14

That is six milk variants on the card. Asianet Newsable also lists Samruddhi with a ₹12 rise but gives no price, so I have left it out of the table rather than guess one.

Why did some Nandini milk go up by ₹12?

Richer milk went up more because KMF prices milk by its fat and SNF, the solids that are not fat. Asianet Newsable reports toned milk at about 3 percent fat, and Shubham and Samruddhi at about 4.5 to 6 percent.

So the ₹8 is the floor and the ₹12 is the creamier end. One honest caveat: Special toned also rose by ₹12, and the reporting gives no fat figure for it. I would not assume every ₹12 packet is the rich one.

Where does the ₹8 actually go?

The government says ₹7 of the ₹8 goes to farmers and ₹1 to the milk unions. PTI reported that the state Cabinet approved that split.

Early reports called the split a proposal; by 2 November it was stated as the decision. The farmer's base procurement price goes from ₹35 to ₹42 a litre, per Nation Press. With the existing ₹5 a litre state incentive, which did not change, the farmer's total goes from ₹40 to ₹47.

The extra ₹1 to ₹4 on the richer variants is explained as fat and SNF, not as a separate split.

How much more will a household spend on milk each month?

A household using one litre of toned milk a day will spend about ₹240 more a month. That is my maths, not a reported figure: ₹8 × 30 days = ₹240.

On Shubham it is ₹12 × 30 days = ₹360 a month, also my maths. Half a litre a day, half the sum.

Does the milk hike mean hotel coffee costs more?

At some hotels, yes: several added ₹3 to ₹5 a cup from 2 November. No hotel body has set a citywide price, and the Bengaluru associations' 5 to 8 percent and 10 to 15 percent figures are estimates. Before the hike, the Bangalore Hotels Association asked the government to hold it to ₹2 to ₹3 a litre, and in Mysuru the hotel owners' association asked for it to be withdrawn. Who estimated what, and what hotels actually did at the counter, is in what happened to Bengaluru's coffee and tea prices.

What happened to the Nandini curd price?

Curd rose by ₹14 at launch: the 1 kg pouch went from ₹54 to ₹68. Reports disagree on the 500 g pouch, so I am not printing a figure for it.

After a public outcry, the Chief Minister said on 2 November that KMF had agreed to cut curd by ₹2 a litre. One report, in Asianet Suvarna, puts the new price at ₹66 a litre. No official start date had been published when I wrote this, and I found no KMF circular. Treat the ₹66 as reported, not confirmed.

Has the milk price hike been rolled back?

No. As of 3 November 2026 the milk prices stand. PTI reported protests demanding a rollback, and the only change announced so far is the curd cut. Milk itself has not moved back.

What should households and restaurants do now?

  • Households: check which variant you actually buy. The rise is ₹8 on toned and ₹12 on Shubham, which is a ₹120 a month difference at a litre a day, by the same maths.
  • Households: if you buy curd, wait for KMF's own revised rate before trusting any single number.
  • Restaurants: work out milk per dish, not milk per month. A coffee uses far less milk than a payasam, and pricing both by the same percentage is guesswork.
  • Restaurants: if you raise prices, say why and say by how much. Diners can see the milk packet price too.

This is the same mechanism as the 2026 sugar price rise: an input moves, and the menu has to decide whether to follow. It also sits next to Karnataka's analogue paneer ban, because when real dairy costs more, the incentive to quietly substitute it goes up with it.

On Edible Factor, a dish shows its price next to its calories, where the restaurant has published them, so a price change sits beside what you are actually eating.

A milk hike is never one price. It is every price with milk in it, arriving on its own schedule.
