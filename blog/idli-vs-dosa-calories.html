The Dosa Won, but the Chutney Nearly Stole It

By the government's own calorie table, one plain dosa is lighter than two idlis. Two tablespoons of coconut chutney carry almost as much as the dosa itself.

By Nikhil Ballal

The 5 minute version

Idli vs dosa calories is the question of which South Indian breakfast carries less energy, and by ICMR-NIN's own table the answer is the dosa. Two idlis come to about 150 kcal. One plain dosa comes to about 125 kcal. Two tablespoons of coconut chutney add about 120 kcal, which is almost a whole dosa.

Most people assume the steamed idli is the lighter choice. Steaming sounds virtuous. Frying on a tawa sounds like a confession. The table does not care how a thing sounds.

So this post is less about idli against dosa and more about everything that sits next to them.

Which is lighter, idli or dosa?

By the NIN recipe, one plain dosa at about 125 kcal is lighter than a serving of two idlis at about 150 kcal.

The numbers come from Annexure 8 of the Dietary Guidelines for Indians, published by ICMR's National Institute of Nutrition in Hyderabad in 2011. Annexure 8 is a table of approximate calorific values of cooked preparations, each with a standard portion beside it. Idli is listed as 2 pieces. Dosa is listed as 1.

One small honesty note. The table just says "Dosa". Masala dosa has its own separate row, so I read the plain row as a plain dosa. That word is mine, not NIN's.

Is one idli lighter than one dosa?

Yes: by the NIN table one idli works out to about 75 kcal and one dosa is 125, so piece for piece the idli is lighter. If you keep one fact from this post, keep that one.

My maths: 150 kcal for 2 idlis, divided by 2, is 75 kcal an idli. One dosa is 125. So a single idli is 50 kcal lighter than a single dosa (125 minus 75).

The dosa only wins because the table compares a plate of two idlis with one dosa, and that is how most of us actually eat them. Nobody has ever ordered one idli and left feeling complete.

So neither "idli is lighter" nor "dosa is lighter" is the full answer. The answer depends on the count, and the count is the part people skip.

How many calories are in coconut chutney?

Two tablespoons of coconut chutney carry about 120 kcal by the NIN table. The same row covers groundnut and til chutney.

NIN's tablespoon is 15 ml, so that is 30 ml of chutney, which is not a lot. It is still 120 kcal against the dosa's 125.

The other chutneys in the same table sit far apart. Tomato chutney is about 10 kcal a tablespoon. Tamarind chutney made with jaggery is about 60 kcal a tablespoon. The side you pick moves more than the main you pick.

What does a full idli or dosa plate add up to?

A full plate with sambar and coconut chutney comes to about 380 kcal for idli and about 355 kcal for dosa, by my maths from the NIN table.

NIN lists sambar at about 110 kcal for one 200 ml cup. Here is the working, all on NIN portions.

  • Idli plate: 2 idlis (150) + 2 tablespoons coconut chutney (120) + 1 cup sambar (110) = 380 kcal.
  • Dosa plate: 1 dosa (125) + 2 tablespoons coconut chutney (120) + 1 cup sambar (110) = 355 kcal.
  • Idli plate with tomato chutney instead: 150 + 20 (2 tablespoons at 10 each) + 110 = 280 kcal.

The idli and dosa plates are 25 kcal apart. Swapping coconut chutney for tomato chutney moves the idli plate by 100 kcal (120 minus 20). By my maths that one swap is four times the size of the whole idli versus dosa debate.

Does a ghee dosa or a bigger idli change the answer?

Yes, and I cannot tell you by how much from this table.

Annexure 8 has no row for ghee, butter or a ghee roast dosa, so I am not going to print a ghee number I do not have. What I can say is that these are NIN's standard recipes and portions. A restaurant dosa brushed with ghee or butter, a paper dosa the length of your forearm, or an idli bigger than the standard one will all move the numbers. So every number in this post is "by the NIN recipe", not "always".

How should I read "per piece" and "per plate"?

Read the unit before the number, every time.

The NIN table mixes units on purpose, because food comes in different shapes. Idli is per 2 pieces. Dosa is per piece. Sambar is per cup, and NIN's cup is 200 ml. Chutney is per tablespoon, and NIN's tablespoon is 15 ml. A calorie number without its unit is just a mood.

A menu line that simply says "idli" does not tell you how many are coming. Count what arrives at the table, not what the menu calls a plate.

What should diners and restaurants do?

  • Diners: count pieces, not plates. Two idlis and three idlis are different breakfasts.
  • Diners: treat chutney as part of the meal. Two tablespoons of coconut chutney is about 120 kcal.
  • Diners: if you want a lighter breakfast, change the side before you change the main.
  • Restaurants: print the piece count next to the dish, and say whether chutney and sambar come with it.
  • Everyone: treat any table number, including these, as a standard recipe, not a reading of your plate.

Nobody needed to fear the idli. They needed to watch the chutney bowl.

Steamed, it turns out, is a cooking method, not a calorie count.
